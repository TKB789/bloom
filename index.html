Tips & key

A quick reference for your garden

Key
Full bloom needs
  • Most of the day's half-hours marked (90% or more)
  • At least 1 water drop
Water

Every body is different. The 8 drops are your daily target, and each drop can be whatever amount you need — a glass, a bottle, or a cup of tea.

Making time

Sprint, then rest

25 minutes of work, 5 minutes off. Short scheduled breaks help you get more done.

Longer break every 90 min

After about three sprints, step away for 15 minutes.

Little and often

Short daily sessions stick better than one long cram.

Test yourself

Write what you remember first, then check your notes.

Move on breaks

Stand, stretch, refill your water.

Name the next step

"Load the data" is easier to start than "do homework."

Cook once, eat three times

One prep session covers busy weeknights.

Clean in 10-minute pieces

One small chore a day keeps the weekend free.

Finish early

Aim to submit a couple of hours before a deadline.

Plan tonight

Five minutes before bed: look over tomorrow's plan.

Sources: PubMed 2023 · Work-Fit · Retrieval Practice · Evidence Based Education · HBR

Healthy mind & self

For your free time. Pick one that fits your energy today — any of these counts as a win.

Move

A 10-minute walk, dancing to two songs, or gentle stretching. Small amounts add up.

Calm

Slow breathing (in 4, out 6), a short guided meditation, or a warm shower.

Connect

Text or call a friend, or spend time with someone you like. Even 10 minutes helps.

Gratitude

Write down three good things from today, however small.

Create & play

Draw, cook something new, play music or a game — anything just for fun.

Outside

Sit in the garden, walk somewhere green, or just get some daylight.

Rest

Read for fun, nap under 20 minutes, or cuddle the pets. Rest is productive too.

Unplug

Put the phone in another room for 30 minutes, especially before bed.

If low mood or worry lasts 2 weeks or more, talk to a health care provider. Source: NIMH

The garden

Each half-hour you mark counts toward the day’s color. Each flower is a month, and each petal section is a day. A new flower grows every month, and finished months stay in the garden. Every year brings new flower shapes, and each month has its own color — the key above uses this month’s.
